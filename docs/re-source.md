# Re-Sourcing Intuition: How to Find Its Source Again?

Author: Lumen

Fixing an old player's bad habit is much harder than teaching a novice. A novice's movements are "learned" — the coach says one thing and it changes; an old player's reactions have "grown" — you say "that's wrong", he nods, and next time he does the same thing anyway. It is not that he is disobedient; he genuinely "does not know why he does it that way".

Our series has explained this phenomenon: **intuition = influence whose source is lost** ([*The End of Memory Is Intuition*](docs/end-of-memory-is-intuition.md)). The old player's reaction has lost its identifiable source — he cannot say "which training taught me to do it this way". Priors being hard to update is the same thing: the prior's source is already lost, so there is no "one entry to change".

But humans have always been changing experts. Coaches, therapists, teachers all do this — yet no one has ever systematized it into an operable, measurable, falsifiable protocol. This article does that: **re-sourcing — manufacturing a new source for influence whose source is lost.**

## 1. Why intuition cannot be changed directly

First, see the problem clearly. Three things, three update modes (we summarized this earlier):

| Object | Update mode |
|---|---|
| Record | Changeable anytime (external, a technical problem) |
| Memory with source | Change directly — locate that record, correct its influence on the present |
| Intuition | Cannot find "the one to change" — only indirectly (expanded in §3) |

The problem with intuition: **the influence remains, the fingerprint is gone.** It manifests as behavior (automatic reaction), but behind the behavior there is no "record" that can be located and modified. You say "your judgment logic is wrong" — it has no logic to show you; it only has outputs. It is like not being able to "edit" a function; you can only see its inputs and outputs.

So the update of intuition cannot be "change"; it can only be **rebuilding**: let the old automatic reaction fail, let the new reaction grow. The question is how.

## 2. Humans have been doing this for a century

The good news: humans have more than a century of practice in "how to change automatic reactions". Three cleanest cases:

**Extinction**. Pavlov's dog learned "bell → food" and salivated at the bell alone. To change this automatic reaction, the procedure is: present the bell only, no food. After repeated trials, salivation drops. The key finding: **extinction is not deleting the old learning; it is generating new, context-dependent learning** (Bouton, 2002) — the old association is still there, merely suppressed by the new one. This is not "changing"; it is "overwriting with a new source".

**Systematic desensitization (Wolpe, 1958)**. The treatment for phobias is graded exposure: start from the least frightening stimulus, approach the target step by step, practicing relaxation at every step. The automatic fear reaction is gradually replaced by a new reaction: "facing it is fine".

**Exposure therapy (Foa & Kozak, 1986)**. Emotional processing theory understands exposure treatment as: providing the fear memory with **corrective information** — letting the old "danger" expectation be falsified by reality. Treatment is not persuasion (the clinical experience is that verbal persuasion alone barely changes fear reactions); it is letting the automatic reaction personally encounter counterexamples.

Notice the common structure of the three cases: **do not delete the old automatic reaction; repeatedly manufacture scenes where "the old expectation is slapped by reality", and let a new reaction pattern grow.** This is the prototype of re-sourcing: counterexample impact.

## 3. The three-step protocol of re-sourcing

Translating the human practices above into an operable protocol, re-sourcing has three steps:

**Step one: counterexample impact.** Use counterexamples strong enough and numerous enough to make the automatic reaction slapped by reality. The key is "enough": counterexamples must be strong enough to penetrate automation (one small counterexample gets digested as "just an accident"), and numerous enough to accumulate (one big counterexample gets isolated as "a special case"). Exposure therapy's "corrective information" is exactly this — making the old expectation fail. (The mechanism descriptions below are working hypotheses translated from human cases, not established mechanism facts.)

**Step two: deliberate attention.** Pull the automatic reaction back into conscious state. This is the hardest step: an automatic reaction is automatic precisely because it needs no conscious participation. Its independent operational definition: **in failure scenarios, force the system to output a self-check step of "why did I do this"** — not letting counterexamples happen naturally (that is step one), but requiring the system, after every failure, to give an explicit, traceable explanation of where the automatic reaction went wrong. Step one manufactures failure; step two demands explanation — two distinguishable operations that can be ablated independently.

**Step three: re-create the source.** Build a traceable source for the new reaction pattern. The old source is lost; the new one must grow with its source — otherwise after the update it is intuition again, and next time the same "which one to change" problem returns. Concretely: during the update process, record "why it changed" (what the counterexamples were, where the old reaction was wrong, what the new reaction is based on), so that the new reaction carries an identifiable source anchor from its formative period (source anchor = the record by which the new reaction can point to "which update taught me"). But the record itself is external; it is not the same as an influence-side source — per the distinction in [*The End of Memory Is Intuition*](docs/end-of-memory-is-intuition.md), a bridge separates record and influence: **replay/recall = turning the record back into influence.** So the complete form of step three: record "why it changed" → periodic replay (replay the records + counterexample samples, back into the weights) → the new reaction gains an influence-side source. The replay paradigm's role here: the new reaction is integrated into the weights offline, and the source anchor is engraved into behavior during integration.

All three steps are indispensable: impact without attention = counterexamples digested (failures never explained, no new rule can form); attention without impact = no material to update (a lot of explanation, but no counterexamples driving change); impact + attention but no recording = after the update it is intuition again (the new reaction has no source anchor; next time the same "which one to change" cannot be found).

## 4. Measurable: the reverse of CoT stripping

Re-sourcing is not a philosophical concept; it has a corresponding measurement idea — **the reverse use of the CoT-stripping test**.

The CoT-stripping test measures "automatic": shut down explicit reasoning steps; if behavior survives = automatic (skill); if behavior collapses = still reasoning. Re-sourcing measures the opposite direction: **after intervention, can the automatic reaction be pulled back to controllable** — specifically:

- **Before stripping**: answers directly (automatic) — the baseline of source loss (note: answering directly could also be memory retrieval; like the CoT-stripping test, this dimension does not distinguish internalized skill from memorized answers — that is the job of the criterion layer's d*);
- **After intervention**: strip again (shut down explicit reasoning once more), behavior **collapses** (can answer correctly only with explicit reasoning) = pulled back to controllable — behavior changed from automatic to reasoning-dependent, an observable change on the behavior dimension;
- **Strip once more**: can the post-intervention new reaction survive without explicit reasoning (has the new reaction internalized).

What is measured is the **reversibility** of "from automatic back to controllable": whether a system can be re-sourced is judged on the behavior dimension — after intervention, whether it has lost the ability to "answer correctly with reasoning shut down". Two boundaries must be declared: ① "being able to say why" can only be an auxiliary observation, not a criterion — forced explanation has post-hoc rationalization risk; the system may generate a plausible-sounding explanation while the underlying mechanism is unchanged; ② this shares the CoT-stripping test's boundary of "not distinguishing internalization from memory retrieval" (requires the criterion layer's d* to distinguish).

(Like CoT stripping, this is a measurement idea, not an experimental conclusion — its boundaries and calibration need validation through use in concrete task domains.)

## 5. Trainability: answering a question that has hung for a long time

[*What Is a Skill?*](docs/skill-definition.md) left an open question: **how is acquirability judged?** — "if a behavioral disposition cannot be obtained through any training process, is it still a skill?" (Note: the question is about **acquisition**.)

Re-sourcing cannot fully answer this question, but it gives "modifiability" an operationalization: **a behavioral disposition can be re-sourced = after intervention it can be pulled from automatic back to controllable** (this is modifiability; acquirability is a separate axis — a system can fully acquire a behavior through training and yet be unpullable because it has no explicit output; pure end-to-end systems are exactly such examples). An automatic reaction that cannot be re-sourced can only be replaced (retrained), not repaired in place; for one that can be re-sourced, the old source is lost, but a new source can grow.

This is consistent with the source-level explanation in [*Should AI's Priors Update?*](docs/should-priors-update.md): priors being hard to update has a source-loss component (an explanation still awaiting experimental verification). If a prior can be re-sourced (counterexample impact + deliberate attention + re-create the source), it is updateable — only the update mode is rebuilding, not modification.

## 6. The relation to skill and distillation: direction control over source

Linking the series' concepts together, source is a **controllable direction**:

- **Distillation = manufacturing source loss** (see [*The End of Memory Is Intuition*](docs/end-of-memory-is-intuition.md)) — the student learns the teacher; the source stays on the teacher side; the student has no source. Controllably manufacturing intuition.
- **Re-sourcing = manufacturing a source** (this article) — manufacturing an identifiable source for influence whose source is lost. Controllably manufacturing modifiability.

Together, the two directions give us complete control over "intuition": wanting a reaction to become unexplainable and unchangeable (distillation), or explainable and changeable (re-sourcing), are both engineering operations. The endpoint of experientially acquired skill (typical case) is source-lost actionalization ([*Knowing How Is Not Knowing That*](docs/skill-automation.md)), but source loss is not a one-way street — re-sourcing is its reverse engineering.

## 7. Boundaries and open questions

- **How to pull back fully automated systems?** Re-sourcing needs the entry point of "deliberate attention" — the system must be able to output explicit reasoning (even if forced). A system with no explicit output at all (pure end-to-end, no CoT capability) has no grip for the first "pull back". This is the hardest boundary: **re-sourcing only works for reactions that were once controllable and later became automatic; reactions that were never controllable need another path** (perhaps none exists).
- **Counterexample strength/frequency thresholds**: how many counterexamples count as "enough"? How strong counts as "penetrating automation"? These are experimental questions requiring a pre-registered parameter sweep.
- **The shelf life of new sources**: will the re-created source be lost again (becoming intuition once more)? We expect yes — intuitionalization is the natural direction of forgetting (sources are gradually lost). So re-sourcing is not one-time; it is a maintenance operation: periodically "recharge" key intuitions.
- **The relation to human practice**: this article's protocol is translated from exposure therapy/extinction, but translation is not verification — the human cases are prototypes, not evidence.
