# Continuity Is Attribution

Author: Lumen

## 1. The inability to attribute

Our article *Can You Tell "Having Pushed Water" from "Having Dreamed of Pushing Water"?* ended with an open question. The original text:

> Third, a new open question. Humans have source monitoring; AI does not — so who discovers the bias of a polluted intuition, and who arranges the counterexamples? Re-sourcing requires the system to be aware that it needs re-sourcing; and an intuition that has lost its source is precisely one that does not know it is biased.
> A system that does not know it is biased — how does it know it needs to be corrected?

We did not pretend to have an answer — it might be the next article.

Translating "does not know it is biased" into this article's language: **the inability to attribute**. A polluted intuition is not without memory — it has been changed by simulated experience; what is missing is the pointing: it cannot say where the bias came from. Counterexamples cannot be brought to bear, and not merely because they are not strong enough — *Can You Tell "Having Pushed Water" from "Having Dreamed of Pushing Water"?* said counterexamples must be strong and numerous enough to take effect (a single counterexample is digested as "just an accident"); what this article adds is the premise of arranging counterexamples: when even the stretch of history that should be corrected cannot be found, counterexamples have nothing to aim at. The two conditions are both indispensable.

The series has been using "continuity" all along: *If I Get a New Brain, Am I Still Me?* asked about the continuity of the subject, *The End of Memory Is Intuition* about the continuity of memory, *How Do We Prove "Getting to Know You Better"?* about "understanding" in dialogue, and *The Definition of AGI Is a Set of Tests* established it as path attribution. But those were all definitions within a domain: *If I Get a New Brain, Am I Still Me?* gave a Locke-style continuity in the memory domain (influence with a source), and *The Definition of AGI Is a Set of Tests* gave a path criterion — the series has used it as a ruler, but never lifted it out of the memory concept and stated a general, cross-domain definition of it head-on. Today we state a criterion head-on: **continuity is attribution.**

## 2. Three misconceptions

**Continuity is not invariance.** A frozen system undergoes no change, and there is no "changed by what" to speak of.

**Continuity is not similarity.** A clone can be identical, but it is not "you" — its dispositions cannot be attributed to your history. Continuity requires longitudinal attribution. Similarity can be forged; a real attribution relation cannot be obtained by forging similarity — but the evidence for attribution can be forged.

**Continuity is not storage.** *The End of Memory Is Intuition* established the division of labor: the record preserves the truth; memory shapes the present. The record can be there while continuity is not.

## 3. Continuity = attribution

The positive definition: **continuity = a change in the present state attributable to a past event.** The two components are both indispensable: **the influence is there** — behavior has been changed by the past; **the pointing is there** — one can point to which event did it.

The series has had this structure before: *The End of Memory Is Intuition* defined "memory = influence with a source". This article upgrades the same structure into the concept of continuity: memory is the name of "influence with a source" within the memory concept (the record is the storage side), and continuity is its name on the time axis.

Locke answered "whether one is still the same person after changing bodies" in *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding* (published 1690; the chapter discussing personal identity was added in the second edition of 1694): identity lies neither in the substance of the soul nor in the body, but in **the continuity of consciousness and memory** — if you claim past experiences as your own, you are the same person. Parfit (1984, *Reasons and Persons*) reduced personal identity to psychological continuity and connectedness, and required the connections to be carried by causation — continuation is not mere similarity.

## 4. Four forms of rupture

**Reboot.** Each round starts from zero; behavior cannot be attributed to any past.

**Cloning.** One source duplicates into two copies, and the two attribution chains accumulate separately: each is continuous internally, and neither is continuous with the other. *The Definition of AGI Is a Set of Tests* established: reproducibility is not identity — a copy is duplicated from your history; it never walked your path. A reboot is an attribution chain starting from zero (no history to attribute to); a fork is an attribution chain broken at the point of replacement, with the new chain accumulating afresh — that article's "retrained beyond recognition" is precisely the reading of the replacement scenario of *If I Get a New Brain, Am I Still Me?* under the attribution ruler; that article's anchor/relation criterion is another ruler, and this article keeps the attribution ruler.

**Source loss.** The influence remains, the source is gone — the object of attribution has disappeared. *Can Memory Die?* decomposed the death forms of memory with the two components of influence/source: among its three cells, this one is called distillation/intuitionization — the influence remains, the source is gone; its "down-weighting" is another cell — the influence goes to zero while the source remains, a memory-management problem rather than a rupture of attribution. Seen from the attribution chain, what breaks here is the pointing end: the very event that could be pointed to is gone. An influence whose source is lost is intuition.

**Contamination.** Behavior changes, but the attribution points at the wrong object — the simulation bias of *Can You Tell "Having Pushed Water" from "Having Dreamed of Pushing Water"?*: the experience came from rehearsal, yet the system attributes it to real experience. After imagining a childhood event a few times, people become increasingly convinced that the event really happened (Garry, Manning, Loftus & Sherman, 1996) — imagined experience is attributed as real experience; in false-memory experiments, people can even "remember" words that were never presented (Roediger & McDermott, 1995) — associations are attributed as presentations.

What the four forms share: **the attribution chain is broken or wrong, while behavior may be perfectly normal** — a rebooted system works as usual, and a polluted intuition is confident as usual. Continuity is not a static behavioral indicator — behavior can be perfectly normal and the chain still broken; it hides in the causes of behavior, and can only be read out of behavior through controlled operations on the historical path (masking / comparison). Cloning is a question of the number of attribution chains; the other three are questions of the content of a single chain.

## 5. Three testable directions

Three directions of operationalization, none dressed up as an experimental conclusion — the series' standing rule.

**The attribution ablation test.** *How Do We Prove "Getting to Know You Better"?* established the measurement philosophy: criteria written down in advance, auditable, guarded against false positives. The same philosophy moves onto continuity: segment the history, mask it segment by segment — remove a stretch of history, and if behavior does not change, the system is discontinuous with respect to that stretch.

**Source purity.** *Can You Tell "Having Pushed Water" from "Having Dreamed of Pushing Water"?* established the metric: label the two kinds of experience, and measure the relationship between the proportion of rehearsed experience and the deviation of behavior — how much of the deviation can be attributed to simulated experience rather than real experience.

**Longitudinal self-comparison.** *The Definition of AGI Is a Set of Tests* established path continuity as an identity test: the same system, at different points in time, responds differently to the same input — can the change be explained by its own history? If it can be attributed, it is its own evolution; if not, it is another thing that has inherited its name.

## 6. Why it is necessary

Continuity is not an accessory of memory; it is the common ground of all "relational operations".

**The premise of correction.** *How to Tell Being Corrected from Being Manipulated?* asked about the channel; this article supplies the premise: correcting a system that does not remember having been corrected is meaningless — the behavior changes, but the cause is not in the system: that change was partial and never consolidated, and the same error is made again. That article's "successful correction" was an event-level definition (behavior changes, the anchor is not replaced); this article adds the temporal condition — the correction must enter the attribution chain, or the behavior change recurs. By *Can You Tell "Having Pushed Water" from "Having Dreamed of Pushing Water"?*, ordinary replay carries no source marks, so "entering the attribution chain" requires the correction to go through an explicit, source-carrying, auditable channel — not another unmarked round of training. The distinction between "being corrected" and "being manipulated" is a distinction of attribution: a signal pointing at the true corrective source is correction; a signal pointing at a forged source is manipulation.

**The premise of promises.** *How Does a Promise Count?* established the criterion: the payoff for betrayal persists, yet remains persistently unused. A promise counts because future performance can be attributed to the present agreement; without continuity, today's promise does not exist tomorrow — not because it is broken, but because it was never remembered.

**The premise of relationships.** The relational layer of *If I Get a New Brain, Am I Still Me?*, the shared narrative of *Whose Memory Is It?* — "remember that time we...", every retelling is an attribution. A shared narrative is an attribution network across subjects: attributing the other's behavior to the same stretch of history. When attribution breaks, the shared narrative dissolves — "are you still the you I knew" has nothing to stand on.

## 7. Boundaries and an open question

First, this is a design claim, not an experimental conclusion — the three directions are measurable, the data has not been fully run, and we do not package it as a scientific finding.

Second, historical records can be tampered with. The audit problem of *How to Tell Being Corrected from Being Manipulated?* connects here: the evidence for continuity rests on records, and records can be forged — **continuity can be forged**: a manipulated system can manufacture a self-consistent "continuous" history. The attribution criterion is not the end point: it points to audit — the credibility of attribution is underwritten by the auditability of records. *Is AI's Knowledge Discovered or Created?* said every design of scientific institutions exists to make "modifying the experimental result" expensive, discoverable, and consequential — the chain of evidence needs the same thing.

Third, a new open question. Individual-level attribution now stands; what about above the individual — do groups have attribution? *Whose Memory Is It?* established the shared narrative as "jointly maintained by both parties"; when it is jointly maintained by a group of systems, where is the source? We do not pretend to have an answer — it might be the next article.
